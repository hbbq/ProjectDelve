# Expedition architecture investigation

The original investigation, [Phase 1 baseline](#phase-1-implementation) and [Phase 2A](#phase-2a-full-wld1) are retained below. [Phase 2B](#phase-2b-persistent-nature-and-spatial-fields) describes the pinned nature and fields. ## Recommendation

The [DelveMart policy](#delvemarts-in-generated-regions) records the current arrival and optional placement guarantees.

Build Expedition as a coordinator over a succession of ordinary Scenarios. The Engine resolves each map independently and emits its existing `GameCompleted` result. The coordinator owns world coordinates, successor generation, Hero transfer and durable saves. Keep WLD1’s construction pipeline and introduce a versioned base-nature sampler plus an explicit entry/exit plan. Establish a protected passage before optional structures can make the exit impossible. Implement resurrection as optional Scenario setup with a narrowly scoped Engine lifecycle extension.

The first implementation can prove the architecture with two adjacent 60 × 60 regions, one party, one Reach Area objective and a real save/reload between regions. Full spatial variation, resurrection and the saved-expedition UI can land independently after that slice. This report proposes details for approval. ## World coordinates and the map handoff

Use a signed `RegionCoordinate(X, Y)` and a fixed positive `RegionSize` per Expedition. With width and height 60, region `(2,0)` has world origin `(120,0)` and covers `120 ≤ X < 180`, `0 ≤ Y < 60`. Region `(-1,0)` covers `-60 ≤ X < 0`. Use mathematical floor for world-to-region conversion, checked multiplication and addition, and a documented axis convention consistent with `Cell` (`+Y` toward the bottom of the board). Keep authored and runtime Cells local to the map. Use long coordinates in the Expedition contract and check the adapter into WLD1’s currently signed 32-bit `WorldWindow`. This supports practical open-ended play. A sampler/API expansion would be necessary to go beyond its numeric domain. Keep stride fixed even if later versions offer another size when starting an Expedition. [WorldWindow validation](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationRecipe.cs#L24) · [Engine Cell](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs#L90)

Record a transition plan before generating a region. It should include the source and destination coordinates, cardinal direction, cross-edge position and width, and the entry and exit footprints needed for generation. A single world seam key identifies both sides of an edge. For an east exit at local `(59, k)`, the destination enters on its west edge at local `(0, k)`. Reserve matching edge Cells and compatible passable approach terrain on both sides. Put the deployment area just inside the incoming approach and the objective exit pad just inside the outgoing approach. The physical transition can be represented by matching board-edge markers. Each map remains its own board with ordinary Engine edge boundaries. Pick landing offsets away from corners, deterministically favor existing natural Land in a small neighborhood of the seam, and use preparation if no suitable offset exists. Preserve the outgoing plan in the source save so a retry, catalog change or generator update cannot move the incoming landing.

For V1, choose one signed horizontal axis and one signed vertical axis from the world seed, then choose between those two forward directions using an Expedition-plan seed derived from the current coordinate. Each step increases the signed coordinate sum by one, which proves the path cannot loop or become trapped by its own visited regions. Keep a visited index and reject any successor that conflicts with it, including when loading a damaged manifest. This is an explicit product tradeoff. Four unrestricted directions plus filtering visited neighbors can trap a self-avoiding walk, and would require a different policy. No region identity or direction choice should depend on map-generation RNG call order, process hash codes, current Content or wall time.

Reuse `ReachArea` with a rectangle near the exit, explicit Unit selectors for all original IDs and `Count` 2 or 3. The Engine already counts any qualifying subset simultaneously at a stable boundary, with whole footprints inside the area. Explicit selectors keep summons and other allies out. Assign No Objective explicitly to other participating Sides if they should never terminate the map through elimination. This detail matters when all originals are absent pending resurrection. Treat the stable `GameCompletion` with the Expedition Side among its achievers as the transition trigger. Publish the old map’s result and then let **Continue Expedition** invoke an idempotent server command. This preserves the completion playback and gives an unambiguous retry point. Never advance on a browser position check, a swept Move through the pad, or an intermediate event snapshot. ## Geography and variable generation settings

### What WLD1 already guarantees

`NaturalTerrainSampler` evaluates forest, rock, openness and soil from absolute coordinates. Its water bodies belong to stable absolute owner squares. The crop scans beyond its bounds, using widened arithmetic for the shoreline halo and a conservative three-owner-square reach. The crop passes `OffsetX + x` and `OffsetY + y` for each local Cell. Identical seed, natural settings and natural-terrain availability therefore give the same sample at the same absolute coordinate, including across signed offsets. A continuous noise field yields spatial coherence, with abrupt boundaries between categorical terrain types where the classification changes. The water features are finite stylized lakes and ribbons. There is no basin, drainage or tributary simulation. [Sampler and water owners](ProjectDelve.Generation/NaturalTerrainGenerator.cs#L9) · [Crop sampling](ProjectDelve.Generation/NaturalTerrainGenerator.cs#L198) · [WLD1 geography](ProjectDelve.Generation/WORLD_GENERATOR.md#L267)

Changing the scalar coverage settings per crop invalidates sample equivalence. Passing latest Content into base sampling can also change existing classifications because `wet-grass` and `rocky-water` depend on catalog availability. Even when the raw samples agree, WLD1 Enrichment, approaches, repair and local construction can change the final Cells. Current stage seeds are derived from a root seed and stage role. Using the same root blindly for every local stage would also reuse its random sequences. Use separate region-derived seeds for local stages. [Natural classification and Content switches](ProjectDelve.Generation/NaturalTerrainGenerator.cs#L17) · [Stage seed derivation](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationPipeline.cs#L249) · [Pipeline ordering](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationPipeline.cs#L23)

| Layer | Proposed inputs and lifetime |
| --- | --- |
| Base natural geography | Expedition world seed, absolute Cell and globally owned features, pinned nature implementation, pinned natural terrain definition/availability set, pinned nature fields. Any requested crop samples the same underlying geography. |
| Local Enrichment | Existing forest, rock, water and grassland passes operating on the sampled crop. Region-derived streams and resolved intensity per stage. Modifications near the seam must preserve the reserved approach contract. |
| Routes, Civilization and Sites | Existing local passes with per-region settings and current permitted Content. May alter the base crop under generation rules. Entry, objective pad and seam protection are supplied before these passes. |
| Population and Loot | Existing independent settings, current Content and separate streams. Run on validated final geometry. Preserve the selected scenario Loot composition and finite supply. |

### Small parameter-field model

Start with a typed Expedition generation profile whose fields mirror `WorldGenerationRecipe` and its nested settings. Each configurable field has a constant default or a versioned field specification with a stable domain key, wavelength or region scale, range and an explicit mapping to its typed setting. Use a small deterministic interpolated lattice with independently hashed values per domain. Constant values preserve WLD1-style configuration. Region controls can sample that lattice at region centers and map to bounded values or enum bands. An independent coordinate hash is sufficient for settings that should vary abruptly. Persist the full profile and field algorithm version, and retain the actual resolved local settings with each region’s provenance. The current pipeline binds the natural step to `WorldDefinition` settings and uses `World.Seed` for local stage streams. Give the Expedition entry point an explicit base sampler input and a separate resolved local seed/settings input. Retain the current recipe adapter for ordinary WLD1. [Typed recipe and validation](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationRecipe.cs#L11) · [Generator plan](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationPipeline.cs#L19)

- Sample Forest and Rock coverage as continuous values at each global Cell, applying the sampler’s terrain priority and conditional land budgets. Map independent channels into a valid continuous coverage budget before classification. At saturation the coverages necessarily compete for the same Cells. Keep the independent inputs and record the mapping. Avoid computing map-local quotas or rescaling based on the sampled crop.
- Evaluate Water coverage as a continuous field at global Cells. The V1 sampler can also evaluate it at stable feature-owner coordinates to set spawn probability and nominal dimensions. Use the per-Cell value to modulate shorelines or ribbon width within bounded reach, and search a fixed halo large enough for that bound. This allows Water coverage to vary across one large feature and within one map. Define the extreme dry/full cases in the pinned algorithm and test long water features crossing steep gradients. Pick Water form per feature owner from its field. An owner may be correlated at regional scales and every crop that touches it must agree on its form. A single crop-level form switch would change an existing water body at the seam.
- Sample local Enrichment intensities, route and preparation controls, Campsite/Settlement/Site settings, Population density, Difficulty and Loot abundance independently at their chosen scales. For settings currently bundled together, retain the typed WLD1 validation and add only the concrete override needed by Expedition. Keep board size, scope revisions and Hero capacity as explicit operational constraints. Preserve independent settings even where accepted placement counts are limited by geometry or budgets. Validate high-density/easy, low-density/hard and plentiful/sparse Loot combinations separately.

### Nature versioning

Pin an executable nature implementation, its field algorithm and serialized parameters, and the effective natural symbol-to-terrain mapping used by that implementation. Keep a small in-process registry of supported nature implementations with versioned constants, hash domains, interpolation/classification helpers and water ownership policy. Share helpers only while their behavior is guaranteed identical for the registered versions. Retain old implementations and golden sample vectors across updates, including seam and extreme-coordinate cases. Fix platform-sensitive math or use reproducible numerics if cross-runtime vectors diverge. Reject an unrecognized nature version for frontier generation with a specific diagnostic. Saved regions remain loadable when their state schema is supported. A version number alongside a call to the latest sampler cannot preserve unexplored geography.

Keep this version boundary at base sampling and its required terrain definitions, including their dependency closure where applicable. The same Type ID cannot represent two different definitions on one map today. Make pinned natural terrain identities authoritative for that map, and validate current local Content against those identities. Pin only the identities required for nature and seam compatibility. Record generator build, Content revision/digest and resolved recipe for local provenance. Existing WLD1 strings lack the field and seam inputs, and WLD1 intentionally evolves in place. Archive the materialized maps and use a distinct Expedition profile for frontier generation. ## Guaranteed progression and construction

### Existing acceptance path

The normal pipeline samples and enriches terrain, reserves a PlayReady deployment and departure, constructs routes and protected Civilization/Sites, performs mandatory repair and finalizes dungeon geometry. Population follows and final Site Loot allocation uses its occupancy. Final materialization verifies Engine legality and actual access from deployment. `ScenarioSetup` transports deployment Cells, placement order, maneuver core, departure and ordinary required-location declarations. It deliberately leaves generator permissions and proofs out of runtime state. [Pipeline execution](ProjectDelve.Generation/WorldGenerationPipeline.cs#L263) · [Deployment reservation and validation](ProjectDelve.Generation/HeroDeployment.cs#L51) · [Setup schema](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioSetup.cs#L5) · [Final access validator](ProjectDelve.Generation/PlayReadyScenarioSetup.cs#L37)

Every protected candidate is checked against the combined accepted/proposed board and its complete declarations. Ordinary required locations use conjunctive groups with `Any` or `All` coverage. The guard rejects optional candidates whose complete permitted graph cannot preserve those obligations. Mandatory repair then seeks a witness on accepted geometry, first through styled low-disturbance, moderate and aggressive passes, then a complete search of simultaneous, declared, monotone operations. Ordinary Land analysis treats normally openable Doors as potential passages. Structural Walls, blocking terrain and Machines are obstacles. Protected interiors can still be ordinary transit. Water-to-Stepping-Stones conversion is already available where declared, and the Engine verifies repaired geometry before publication. A failed candidate never becomes available for a later repair to fix. [Completion guard](ProjectDelve.Generation/CompletionGuard.cs#L87) · [Conversion permissions](ProjectDelve.Generation/ConstructionCapabilities.cs#L33) · [Repair and Engine witness validation](ProjectDelve.Generation/MandatoryConnectivityRepair.cs#L58)

### Recommended guarantee

1. Specify both seam approaches and the exit pad before protected construction. Extend the deployment planner with an Expedition start anchor/allowed band and retain PlayReady’s capacity, open internal Edges, maneuver core and departure requirements. Establish a distinct required location for the exit before the first completion-guarded construction. Reserve the approach and pad, including their usable Cells and internal/approach Edges. Choose at least the objective count of simultaneous legal standing positions. Verify all required exit positions, plus legal initial deployment for the resolved party.
2. Find an ordinary path on the enriched, still-unowned board. Try existing open movement, then ordinary permitted preparation in preference order. Apply deterministic weighted cardinal search with stable total ordering of ties, and record the chosen path. Reserve its Cells and transitions as a passage. Compatible roads and read-only crossings may share it. Optional structure placement must preserve passage and other obligations. Exclude initial blocking placements from this specific witness so the delivered map has a guaranteed physical approach even at setup. Keep ordinary PlayReady repair for the full required-location set after construction.
3. If the earlier steps cannot establish the passage, construct it across the still-unowned board using a catalog of valid ordinary Land conversions, including saturated Water/Rock if required. This is the proposed **constructive alternative to late emergency carving**. It has the same entry-to-exit guarantee if a contiguous writable set is established before any blocker can claim it. Protect the exit, entry and passage from later blocking geometry, Machines and initial Population. In the emergency case, make the reserved path open throughout so its existence does not depend on a particular Hero’s interaction options. Optional settlements and routes can be placed elsewhere or omitted with diagnostics. Keep all previously accepted required locations and verify their ordinary access. Never reuse a rejected optional candidate or substitute a seed.
4. Run ordinary PlayReady validation on the delivered geometry after Population and Loot, before party placement. `HeroDeploymentPlanner.Validate` rejects any footprint in the deployment area, including the intended Heroes. Follow Quick Play’s pre-party validation sequence. Then materialize the full Scenario with the party, validate Engine setup, and independently verify its recorded witness against actual Engine Land movement, including each Cell/Edge, the required access set and the full exit pad. If a final combined authoring verdict is needed, explicitly account for the intended original placements in that check. The Expedition reservation also prevents initial hostile footprints on the witness. Subsequent battles and temporary Unit occupancy are gameplay. If the witness is invalid in final materialization, report a generator defect and keep the current Expedition checkpoint. [Pre-party occupancy check](ProjectDelve.Generation/HeroDeployment.cs#L176) · [Quick Play validation and placement](ProjectDelve.Web/WorldQuickPlay.cs#L40)

A late emergency override would need a coordinated transaction over geometry and owner metadata. Structural Walls and Dungeon shells have owned Edges/Cells, entrance/room/corridor descriptors, necessary approach roots, legal interfaces and reservations. Removing a Machine also affects its identity, operating positions and required-location groups. Terrain assemblies and resources can invalidate neighboring layers. Existing completion declarations are immutable once accepted. A safe late variant would need a producer to rebuild the affected uncommitted structure as a whole, recalculate permissions, interfaces and obligations, and validate the combined patch before Population and Loot. It would require an explicit change to the current WLD1 protection contract if it can revise accepted declarations. A raw Cell/Wall punch after Population is unsafe. The early constructive passage avoids a second family of structure editors and gives a clear proof obligation. [Construction ownership and route reservations](ProjectDelve.Generation/BoardPlanning.cs#L343) · [Interactive access checks](ProjectDelve.Generation/PlayReadyScenarioSetup.cs#L125) · [PlayReady invariants](CONSTRAINTS.md#playready1--mandatory-wld1-geographic-contract)

Preserve the existing diagnostic distinction between proven impossible, incomplete analysis, witness mismatch, invariant failure and invalid final materialization. Include Expedition/region/seam, exact seeds and versions, stage and board revision, relevant owners, path/witness, ordered conversions, guard rejections and the final Engine validation result. Record an emergency-intervention count and geometry summary in generation diagnostics. Send players a retryable transition error without omniscient geometry. A missing search proof or a time budget exhausted warrants an incomplete diagnostic. A valid input that fails to deliver the reserved route is a generator defect. ## Scenario resurrection

### Area and setup contract

Introduce one small immutable `CellArea` value for an explicit set of board-local Cells. At Scenario creation, validate that the set is nonempty, distinct and inside the board, and canonicalize it by increasing Y then X. Keep membership and ordered enumeration available to Engine queries. Publish it as an optional `Resurrection` Scenario declaration with the area and explicit initial Unit Instance IDs. Validate eligibility against resolved initial identities after placement, with diagnostics pointing to the authored fields. Use an additive JSON property omitted for existing Scenarios. Older servers use strict unmapped-member handling and will reject new Scenarios that actually enable the feature. In Expedition, derive this area from `ScenarioSetup.Deployment.Cells` and validate that they match. Deployment’s `PlacementOrder` remains its own deterministic placement policy. Adapt its existing Cell list to the same value internally without changing version-1 setup JSON. Keep existing rectangle-based Reach Area and Orders contracts for V1. A future arbitrary-shape objective can share the membership check with an explicit additive payload when required. ### Engine lifecycle

`ResolveDefeat` already retains the same Unit record at zero HP, removes its Figure, places the same inventory objects on the ground, records defeat and queues native When Defeated consequences. `ResolutionEvents.Add` emits the outcome and calls the ordinary Loot drop path. The FIFO can pause at dice decisions for a consequence, and the Engine then completes attack follow-ups. Undying replaces defeat and therefore supplies no resurrection trigger. Use this single actual-defeat entry to queue one resurrection per eligible ID. A small dedicated state record suffices for queued returns that survive a dice boundary and for approved deferred placement. Include it in state copying, authoritative JSON and continuation validation. [Lifecycle and consequences](ProjectDelve.Engine/GameEngine.cs#L745) · [Event-time snapshot and Loot](ProjectDelve.Engine/GameEngine.cs#L814) · [Continuation order](ProjectDelve.Engine/GameEngine.cs#L278)

At an approved resolution boundary, find the first legal board-order anchor using normal Engine placement queries, matching the complete Figure footprint against the area, terrain acceptance, Edges, Machines, occupancy, Unique limits and effective permissions of the returning Unit. Evaluate this automatic placement against the complete board, using the Unit’s current empty inventory after defeat. For multiple queued IDs, process one deterministic order, proposed actual defeat FIFO, with each successful placement updating occupancy for the next. Restore the retained Unit in place, set `CurrentHp` to `UnitType.Hp` (the current maximum in today’s model), and recreate its Figure Upright. `PlaceUnit` creates a new record and rejects a retained ID. Share its legality checks through a concrete re-entry path. Preserve Side, Group, Type, original ID, the Unit’s own usage counters and all surviving Units’ `SummonerUnitId`. The dropped Equipment stays on the board with its existing counters. The current map’s frozen objective IDs remain intact. [Placement](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs#L458) · [HP and supported stats](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs#L191) · [Summon creator rules](CONSTRAINTS.md#summon-creator-identity-and-limits)

The ordering must work for active-unit defeat, including Defeat Self and an enemy reaction during the Unit’s own resolution. Finish ordinary defeat consequences and complete/close the defeated active Unit’s activation before it can re-enter. Split completion bookkeeping from objective evaluation where needed so resurrection runs at the approved boundary before all Sides are evaluated. Keep the declared defeat result and any valid effects tied to that result. Do not mutate the bag, redraw tokens, expand the drawn token’s captured membership or clear its completion history. A revived original may participate when its already scheduled token is drawn if eligible under ordinary rules. If its token was absent at round start or has been spent, it waits for the next ordinary opportunity. A captured but unused member that dies and returns before selection needs an explicit rule. The narrow choices are to keep its existing eligibility or to exclude it from the rest of the drawn token. Either choice must prevent an active self-defeat from reopening the same activation. ## Party transfer and Content refresh

The live Party roster supplies deployment order and stable Unit IDs. A new Expedition starts with its five founders. A successful recruitment appends the new member immediately. The coordinator requests a landing for the current member count, validates the IDs and placements against generated content, and writes ordinary `UnitPlacement` entries with each member's Level and carried inventory. The successor starts before Round 1. The archive preserves the previous board, including its summons and activation state. [Party transfer](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionParty.cs) · [Scenario creation](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioCreation.cs)

Each member carries the physical objects in their authoritative `Inventory` at completion. This includes objects picked up from another Hero. Objects still on the ground stay on the archived board. Defeated members return under the same handoff rules as living members. Each returns Upright at Effective Max HP. Native and retained Equipment Uses refresh to the maxima for their respective Levels. An unused Single Use Item keeps its identity across the handoff. [Ability accounting](ProjectDelve.Engine/AbilityAccounting.cs) · [Ownership validation](ProjectDelve.Engine/Loot.cs)

Capture one latest published `CanonicalContent.Snapshot` per map build, including its revision. Resolve each carried identity `(family, Type ID)` to the whole latest definition, falling back to the prior region’s saved definition only for a missing identity. Validate the combined dependency closure through the existing typed composition boundary. For a missing definition that refers to another missing dependency, include the saved dependency when valid. If a currently available dependency is incompatible, surface the specific conflict. Keep one selected value per identity. The current game setup already captures Unit and Equipment/Item definitions and dependents in `GameState`. Use that state for fallback plus a frozen effective provider for each archived Scenario. Pin the original typed profile independently of ambient Designer workspace drafts unless there is an explicit product requirement to include them. ## Persistence, replay and session integration

### Boundaries already available

`ScenarioDefinitionJson.Materialize` parses and validates initial Scenario setup and produces a fresh `GameState`. DELVE1 packages that setup with gzip and size limits. Canonical references in an authored Scenario can still resolve against the provider at import, so Scenario JSON by itself does not freeze all published Content or represent a running game. Existing direct `GameState` JSON round trips preserve board mutations, retained Units, definition lists, inventory and piles, pending decision and resolution continuations, activation and exploration. Existing Web tests construct a fresh `PlaytestGame` around a restored authoritative `GameState`. The live host creates only a singleton running game and its constructor keeps the default Scenario definition separately, so restoring the state alone can leave restart/context metadata wrong. Normal import and restart create new games with `NewGameContent`. [Scenario JSON](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioDefinitionJson.cs#L8) · [DELVE1](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioDefinitionTransport.cs#L9) · [GameState model](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs#L440) · [Web resume test](ProjectDelve.Web.Tests/ContentEditorWorkflowTests.cs#L95) · [Live host creation](ProjectDelve.Web/PlaytestHost.cs#L44) · [Reset execution](ProjectDelve.Web/PlaytestGame.cs#L262)

The Web runner serializes work, checks session/revision/decision IDs, and publishes event snapshots mid-command. `Snapshot()` reads the latest publication, which may be a transient frame. The save path should run through the same single-writer boundary and take the authoritative stable state, matching active definition, stable revision, mode/session metadata and random continuation together. A saved decision can be awaiting input or dice while an Engine sequence is in progress because the continuation is serialized. Persist from a completed runner boundary such as `stable`, after verified state acceptance, rather than from an observer callback. On load validate snapshot schema and continuation invariants. Restore with a fresh live session/decision ID and regenerated authoritative choice data at the correct pending boundary, without automatically advancing or consuming RNG. A normal browser response is FoW-filtered, pruned and strips the deck. It is unsuitable as save data. ### Storage and atomic transition

Use a separate server-local Expedition store, outside bundled Scenario Library and static assets. Adopt the repository’s bounded read, validated ID, exclusive writer and write-next-to-destination, flush and atomic rename patterns. Define a versioned head and separate immutable, optionally compressed checkpoint files. Avoid rewriting the region payloads when saving one map. A manifest with linear history metadata is sufficient initially. Archive read access in the store can exist independently of a gameplay revisit feature. [Scenario Library storage](ProjectDelve.Web/ScenarioLibrary.cs#L358) · [Atomic persistence](ProjectDelve.Web/ScenarioLibrary.cs#L414) · [Canonical atomic write](ProjectDelve.Web/CanonicalContent.cs#L174)

| Record | Suggested V1 contents |
| --- | --- |
| Expedition head | Schema and storage revision, ID/name/timestamps, world seed, stride, pinned nature and field profile/payload, original roster, active region/checkpoint, ordered transition records and visited region references. Optional statistics belong in metadata. |
| Region checkpoint | Coordinates, entry and prospective exit plan, resolved local settings and provenance, immutable initial Scenario with its effective Content provider or validated minimal materialization closure, full current/final authoritative GameState, gameplay RNG continuation and replay data through the same checkpoint revision. Keep the initial game state or a faithful way to recreate it using the frozen provider. |
| Handoff receipt | Transition ID from Expedition ID, source checkpoint/revision and outgoing seam, old and new checkpoint references, selected Content revision and the transferred IDs/inventory summary. Extract current ownership from the source state. A summary must agree with the committed states and does not become another live inventory. |
| Active selection | Single host-local active Expedition ID, if it should resume after host startup. Keep the active selection a small atomic pointer. Both Expeditions remain resumable if a switch is interrupted. |

On **Continue Expedition**, compare the expected source head revision and the authoritative completion, then capture or load the final source checkpoint. Generate, compose and validate the next Scenario and initial state as detached data. Write both immutable checkpoint references and their data, flush, then publish one new head naming the archived source, successor and receipt. Recheck the expected revision under the writer lock before committing. A process crash before head replacement leaves the old completion resumable. A crash after replacement means the successor is authoritative, including when the acknowledgement never reached the browser. Retry the same transition ID by inspecting the committed receipt. A stalled or failed generation leaves the completed map and its inventory intact. Unreferenced files may be reclaimed later under the store lock. Keep the source completion durable before attempting advancement. Document and test the target filesystem’s rename/durability behavior and verify data integrity on load. If stable boundaries are auto-saved, batch writes as needed and define the advertised recovery guarantee. A manual save must report success only when the checkpoint is committed.

If a retry occurs **before** any successor commits, the same plan, world seed, pinned profile and selected Content produce the same result under the same local pipeline. Clarify whether restart/retry must hold the initial attempt’s candidate Content revision or can select the latest revision again. For exact retries across process restarts, persist a prepared input record with the provider snapshot before building, or reuse a validated staged successor. Keep the previous head authoritative until the commit. A broken schema, unavailable version, invalid provider or no disk space must leave a readable last committed map. Never mark a next region visited until it commits.

Expedition saves will add party, controller/replay and pinned Content overhead. Budget for linear growth and measure populated 60 × 60 checkpoints before choosing per-region limits and autosave frequency. The Scenario Library’s shared 64 MB limit and full-document replacement would couple unrelated saves to that growth. ### Determinism is three contracts

1. **Frontier generation** uses pinned base nature and fields, an explicit seam plan, and local stage seeds derived from the region. Later local generation/Content can evolve. A committed successor is loaded from materialized artifacts, including after updates.
2. **Continuation** uses the full Engine checkpoint plus its own serializable gameplay RNG algorithm and state. Current Web `SystemRandomProvider` and the default World shuffle use `Random.Shared`. A world seed or serialized `GameState` cannot resume that random stream. Supply an Expedition gameplay provider covering token draw, combat dice, d6/d8, Loot draw and World shuffle. Existing Loot draw can use its injected d6s. Keep game entropy independent from shape, stage, and session IDs. Commit the RNG checkpoint alongside state, and restore both on a failed authoritative command. [Web random provider](ProjectDelve.Web/SystemRandomProvider.cs) · [Injected randomness contract](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs#L742) · [World shuffle](ProjectDelve.Engine/WorldResolution.cs#L63)
3. **Deterministic replay** starts with the saved region setup, pinned definitions and initial gameplay RNG, and applies committed inputs in order. Capture submitted and externally or automatically selected decisions at the authoritative provider boundary, plus enough random information to check identical resolution. Record explicit randomness outcomes or a pinned RNG plus verifiable input position/checkpoints. Keep transitions as explicit recorded handoffs to saved region artifacts. Replay the same rules/compatibility version and check results and event-time FoW. A bounded SSE journal is for reconnect, and Gambit observations are filtered. Neither is the authoritative replay source. Long-term replay across changed Engine rules would require preserving compatible Engine behavior or storing event-time playback artifacts. Define the supported compatibility window now. Full checkpoint resume and historical playback across rule changes carry different costs. [Runner journal](ProjectDelve.Web/PlaytestGame.cs#L479) · [Existing replay trace test](ProjectDelve.Engine.Tests/AttackQueryReuseTests.cs#L227)

[Actual journey test](ProjectDelve.Web.Tests/ExpeditionJourneyTests.cs) · [Persistence and failure injection](ProjectDelve.Web.Tests/ExpeditionPersistenceTests.cs)

Each `expedition-<id>` directory contains a versioned `head.json` and SHA-256-named JSON checkpoints. Old byte-identical checkpoints are immutable, including the terminal source named by the receipt. Reads cap the head at 2 MiB and each checkpoint at 32 MiB. The store checks the active payload digest, validates the transition chain and validates the decoded Engine state. It does not rewrite or remove archives when a new region commits. The source completion becomes a committed checkpoint before any successor work. Successor blobs and a head containing the receipt are flushed before publication. Files are staged in their destination directory and the head is installed by same-directory replacement under a writer lock and revision check. Windows uses write-through moves. The Unix path flushes the destination directory after rename. Integration tests use process-interruption probes before and after the head replacement on Windows. This establishes the recovery boundary under test and does not physically simulate power loss. ## Phase 2B persistent nature and spatial fields

### Version boundary and parameter mapping

New saves record **`ExpeditionNature1`**, **`ExpeditionFields1`** and **`SignedCellXY-RightDown1`** in the version-2 head along with the world seed, WLD1 baseline recipe, nature settings, wavelengths and amplitudes. Region identities remain signed `long` values. Coordinates use the fixed 60-Cell stride, with X growing right and Y growing down. The adapter accepts only whole regions that fit the signed 32-bit WLD1 Cell window and rejects out-of-range coordinates before multiplying or narrowing. [Profile and sampler](ProjectDelve.Generation/ExpeditionNature.cs) · [Coordinate adapter](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionWorld.cs)

The pinned sampler owns its V1 hash, signed lattice, interpolation and classification. It samples only absolute Cell coordinates and the saved profile. No statistics or crop boundaries enter the calculation. Broad fields use three octaves with weights `0.60`, `0.30`, `0.10` and wavelength multipliers `1`, `0.30`, `0.075`. The default nature wavelengths are therefore 240, 72 and 18 Cells. Regional control wavelengths are 360, 108 and 27 Cells. Fine, Balanced and Broad also change the distinct shorter terrain classifiers. Water uses smooth lake masks and river-like ribbon masks according to `WaterForm`. V1 chooses the Auto form from the world seed. The masks are stylized terrain and carry no drainage simulation. [Pinned equations and role validation](ProjectDelve.Generation/ExpeditionNature.cs)

| Input | V1 application |
| --- | --- |
| Forest, Rock, Water | Evaluate three independent fields per Cell. Multiply each baseline percentage by `1 + CoverageVariation × (2 × field − 1)`. The default maximum relative swing is 75%. The configuration permits 0–100%. Preserve exact disabled and saturated coverage and proportionally normalize the three budgets if their sum exceeds 100%. Actual category counts depend on the terrain masks and priority, so the budgets are spatial controls rather than crop quotas. |
| Enrichment, Civilization/Sites, Routes, Population density, Difficulty, Loot abundance | Sample six independent domains at the floor of the region center. Round `(2 × field − 1) × RegionalVariation` to a signed step, then clamp to the relevant existing WLD1 range. The default maximum is one step and configuration permits zero through four. Stages and densities explicitly set to disabled, `None` or `Off` stay disabled. The saved Loot preset selects the deck and remains the same even when abundance varies. |
| Current catalog and WLD1 stages | The baseline uses fixed Grass, Dirt, Tree, Solid Rock and Water roles. Before materialization, the adapter checks that current definitions retain their expected direct gameplay meanings. Presentation can change. Downstream terrain and destruction results such as Gravel can use current Content. Enrichment, Sites, routes, Monsters and Loot use the currently installed WLD1 stages and Content. |

Later stages receive resolved local controls and an independent deterministic region seed. The persistent sampler retains the Expedition world seed. This separates the call order and policy of evolving stages from the world fields. A final map also depends on its entry/exit plan, current WLD1 implementation and current compatible Content. Construction guards and initial Monster-footprint checks still gate success. Ordinary WLD1 continues to use its own recipe and sampler. A shorter-scale field supplies a preferred anchor. The opportunities are independent of terrain, settlements, camps, ruins and landmarks. Phase 4 schedules the request after all earlier Site profiles and Beacons, using the same bounded Civilization placement and completion budgets. The overall Civilization switch governs the stage. Earlier Expedition heads retain their stored version, and successor generation continues to use current published Content. It verifies every boundary Edge and the gate endpoints before the candidate can publish. The outpost owns the courtyard, apron and perimeter protection records. ### Phase 4 measurements

In 48 paired default-recipe regions for seeds 17, 42 and 889, Phase 4 requested 30 outposts and placed 29. The rejected request was seed 889, region `(5, 10)`, visit 16. Every sampled plot conflicted with existing geometry or protection, and the final Scenario fingerprint matched its Phase 3 counterpart. All 48 Phase 3 baseline fingerprints matched the archive. Fourteen outposts appeared in regions without living settlements. Examples include a gate one Cell from a primary route, a cleared forest plot and an open-terrain outpost.

With initial Monster footprints blocked, all 48 regions passed occupied Expedition endpoint and deployment checks. Both Door thresholds remained reachable at all 29 outposts. Ten compounds had zero initial Monsters. The others had two to four. The ordinary Site Loot pass placed 23 exploration markers in guardhouse Rooms.

Median Debug generation time rose from 3.77 to 4.13 seconds per region. Median Civilization time rose from 0.75 to 0.85 seconds. Mean completion-guard work rose from 25,686 to 27,770 units. Timings are observations from the paired parallel-seed export.

Deployment and controller assignments remain independent of the balance. A retry builds from the same completed source or returns the existing transition receipt. ## DelveMarts in generated regions

`ExpeditionStations1` is independent of the recorded nature, field and Civilization construction versions. New Expeditions enable it at creation. Reopening an older Expedition loads its materialized active region. Its first successful frontier handoff publishes the Station policy with the new head. Failure leaves the prior checkpoint and policy available for retry.

Region 0 has no guaranteed arrival DelveMart. Starting at region index 1, the generator places the guaranteed Machine during protected deployment construction, before routes or Sites. It prepares a small grass pad adjacent to the maneuver core, keeping the Machine outside deployment Cells, required endpoints and the departure. Party setup assigns the arriving Side as owner and checks for a legal Action-timed Operate source in the actual arrival formation. If refreshed Hero content requires a different operator, setup can exchange two starting positions after validating the complete placement. An incompatible party produces a setup diagnostic before the new head is published. The guarantee also applies when Civilization is disabled.

In every region, including region 0, a separate region-seeded draw requests at most one public DelveMart with probability 1/12. The planner tries at most 96 locations. It requires a connected, passable 5 × 5 pad clear of reservations, route corridors, entrances and internal Edges. The ordinary Machine placement and completion checks accept or omit the candidate. Population checks preserve operating access with initial Monster footprints occupied. The chance of an actual placement is consequently at most 1/12.

Ordinary WLD1 retains its existing Machine selection. Authored Sites and scenarios can explicitly select the Station from the canonical Machine catalog. [Placement code](ProjectDelve.Generation/ExpeditionStationGenerator.cs) · [Playable workshop](VISION_AND_MACHINES.md#play-the-workshop)

## Expedition introductory Population

### Ordinary inputs and placement

The default recipe has Medium density and difficulty 3. Each control samples its own field at the floor of the region center, rounds `(2 * field - 1) * RegionalVariation` to the nearest integer (halves away from zero), adds it to the baseline and clamps the result to the enabled bands. At the default regional wavelength of 360 Cells, the field combines scales of 360, 108 and 27 Cells with weights 60%, 30% and 10%. An explicit Off stays disabled.

On a 60 × 60 board, Low, Medium and High respectively target 16, 36 and 65 encounters, with Unit ceilings of 80, 144 and 256. Density also influences room occupation, compound sizes and patrol requests. Legal space, connectivity, encounter spacing and bounded placement work determine actual totals. Wilderness candidates use 12 × 12 Cell patches with placement weights 0, 2 or 10 (20%, 40% and 40% of patch draws). Sites and road patrols get placement opportunities before wilderness encounters.

Wilderness draws a lone Monster or a clustered group of two to five of the same Type. Selection weights for a Type are its relative weight times 16, 8, 3, 1 or 1 for a difficulty-to-threat difference of 0 through 4. Every available pooled Type can therefore occur even at difficulty 1. Buildings, camps, dungeon rooms and road patrols have additional role, size and composition rules. Ordinary deployment protections reserve Cells and keep required destinations accessible with figures on the board. Several Skeleton Archers can still share the first encounter within Attack or approach distance.

### New-run policy

New runs record `populationVersion: "ExpeditionPopulation1"` on the world profile. After `ExpeditionFields1` samples the geographic center and resolves the normal Population settings, the Expedition adapter adjusts only the Population step. The signed region coordinates give the zero-based index `i = |X| + |Y|` because both forward directions are fixed for the journey. Saves and retries do not advance it.

| Index | Effective difficulty for ordinary bands 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | Highest density | Arrival points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 | Low | 2 |
| 1 | 1, 1, 2, 2, 2 | Medium | 3 |
| 2 | 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 | High | Unrestricted |
| 3 | 1, 2, 3, 3, 4 | High | Unrestricted |
| 4 onward | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | High | Unrestricted |

For indices 0–3 the integer formula is `1 + floor(((normalDifficulty - 1) * i + 2) / 4)`. Density uses the lesser of the ordinary resolved band and the listed cap. An absent or Off Population stays disabled. From index 4 the adapter passes ordinary Population through directly. Profiles without this field also use the ordinary path, including during legacy frontier upgrades.

Each candidate encounter in the first two regions is checked before Engine placement. Take the minimum Manhattan distance from each candidate Monster’s footprint to the Party's ordered deployment Cells, including the Station operator. Within `max(8, base RNG + 3)`, count one point per Monster or two for base RNG above 1. Only a fully accepted encounter consumes its points from the shared allowance. This applies across room, camp, patrol and wilderness requests. It reads no Hero inventory, combat state or play history. Population can affect encounter Loot and final Site Loot positions through ordinary occupation and supply rules.

## Discoverable paid Machines

`ExpeditionEconomy1` gives each region two independent 1-in-12 requests, one each for public DelveCare and Meteor-O-Mat. Their seed streams are separate from Population, Loot, Beacons and DelveMarts. Placement runs after Population and Loot. Each request tries up to 96 Cells for an open 3 × 3 site with an accessible operating approach and a route around the Machine. The generator preserves reserved Cells, route corridors, existing objects and occupied-access guarantees. A failed placement is omitted. A missing vending Item in the region's content also omits the vendor.

New Expeditions record the policy in their world profile. A legacy Expedition adopts it when a new region is successfully published. Archived regions and the currently materialized map keep their saved Machines. The public DelveMart roll is independent of these services. The guaranteed arrival DelveMart starts in region 1. ## Levels at region handoff

The active state carries an ordered `Party` of stable member IDs, its Side and capacity. A fresh Expedition starts with the original five and capacity six. The head retains its founder IDs as provenance. The region's Reach Area reads current Party membership and requires two living members. The checkpoint stores an optional selected recruit separately from a Manage draft. Resume of a legacy five-Hero region verifies the saved digest, then derives the Party from its founders in memory. Archived bytes remain historical.

Successor generation sizes deployment from the current member count. A six-member Party receives six legal ordered slots while keeping the landing approach, exit route and maneuver room. The next region restores each member through the ordinary arrival rules, including defeated members and their still-owned objects. The first-version recruitable Tycoon fits the ordinary single-Cell Land deployment. [Phase 6](ECONOMY_PHASE_6.md)

A checkpoint stores the materialized Type definitions, Unit Levels, Equipment Levels and physical source counters. Reopening a checkpoint resumes those values. At a new region, each Party member's stable ID selects its previous Level and inventory. Retained Equipment keeps its physical ID and Level. Existing arrival restoration gives the Unit its level-specific Effective Max HP, including retained Equipment, and restores its native and Equipment Ability uses. Ground objects stay in their archived region.

A refreshed definition must support the carried Level. If it no longer does, preparation fails before publication. The completed source checkpoint remains available for retry after content is corrected. Definitions missing altogether follow the existing saved-definition fallback. See [Phase 4](ECONOMY_PHASE_4.md).
