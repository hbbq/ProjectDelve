# Project Delve economy and progression investigation

**Recommendation for approval.** Build on three existing boundaries. Resolve Currency on arrival with Loot, use the DelveMart's pending Operate for sales and upgrades, and make Party membership an explicit input to Expedition handoff and its Reach Area objective. Recruitment merits its own Operate confirmation at the same Station because it places a figure and changes the objective population. The largest prerequisite for upgrades is an Engine query for effective maximum HP.

### Agreed design intentions

- Every Side shares one non-negative integer Currency balance. Any Unit can collect board Currency for its Side. All Side balances cross region boundaries. Uncollected amounts stay on the archived board. Coal, Fruit and other Resources keep their own rules.
- An actual defeat can produce at most one ordinary random reward, either unopened Loot or Currency. Physical objects already owned by the defeated Unit still follow ordinary defeat rules.
- The Station can sell individual Items and Equipment and redistribute objects in one transaction. Sales can finance upgrades on the same Confirm. Levels belong to physical Equipment and individual Units. Each Type declares its own finite progression and restoration options.
- The five original Heroes found the Party. Recruitment adds permanent members by Unit ID. Summoning confers no membership. Any two living Party members, including a new recruit, can satisfy the Expedition exit together at the normal evaluation boundary.
- Currency is a working name. The specific prices, level entries, service choices and recruitment limits below are proposals. The existing rules authority still specifies redistribution and the original-five handoff. ## Currency on the board and on defeat

**Implemented today.** Cell Markers have one slot per Cell. The current arrival handler consumes those markers and applies Banana Peel posture effects. Unopened Loot has its own nonblocking collection, may stack, and reserves cards in a finite draw pile. Arrival hazards resolve before mandatory Loot. Ordinary Move resolves at the destination, Stay queues pickup, external placement and Swap queue the affected Units, and Run Rails drains pickup between individual steps. The first Round also queues the initial figures in board order. Known Items and Equipment instead have optional Free Action pickup. [Cell markers and arrival](ProjectDelve.Engine/CellMarkers.cs) · [Round start and movement](ProjectDelve.Engine/GameEngine.cs) · [Rails](ProjectDelve.Engine/RailResolution.cs) · [Loot resolution](ProjectDelve.Engine/LootResolution.cs)

**Proposed representation.** Add optional initial Side balances and positive Currency amounts per Cell to scenario setup. Materialize a Side-ID-to-balance map in `GameState` and one amount per occupied Currency Cell. Merge drops on the same Cell. Keep them independent of Cell Marker, Resource, ground object and Loot reservation slots. They can coexist with a Peel or unopened Loot and have no effect on route legality, placement, LOS, or inventory capacity. Use tokens or denominations on the Cell and a physical bank per Side. Validate board bounds, amounts, Side references and checked arithmetic. Missing balances read as zero. Include both collections in detached state copies and snapshots. [Scenario fields](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioDefinition.cs) · [State and copy boundary](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs)

**Pickup ruling needed.** “Entering” could include each Cell crossed along a path, or the footprint at a resolved destination. Recommend the latter, including Stay, initial placement at first Round, summons, recruitment, external relocation and each resolved rail step. This preserves atomic Move and avoids charging through intermediate Cells to collect Currency. After hazards and their consequences, process surviving arrivals in the existing order, actor first for simultaneous arrivals. Scan occupied Cells top-to-bottom then left-to-right. On a Cell with both rewards, credit its Currency first, then resolve one Loot card and its consequences. Re-read the living collector's footprint before continuing. Thus a Peel can leave a living Lying collector eligible, and relocation or defeat during a card can leave later Cells uncollected. A drop alone never initiates an arrival. Approval of this ordering matters even though today's Immediate cards heal. [Loot timing rules](CONSTRAINTS.md#drops-and-unopened-loot) · [Arrival tests](ProjectDelve.Engine.Tests/LootResolutionTests.cs)

**Defeat and RNG.** Today actual defeat moves owned objects to the former anchor Cell. Emitting `UnitDefeated` then invokes `ResolveLootDrop`. It makes **zero** RNG calls at zero Loot chance or with no unreserved card. Otherwise it rolls one d6 and emits `LootDropRolled` even on failure. Loot identity is drawn later. A replacement such as Undying bypasses the defeat and drop. Scenario Loot overrides take precedence over the resolved Unit Type, including explicit zero. [Defeat path](ProjectDelve.Engine/GameEngine.cs) · [Drop and draw](ProjectDelve.Engine/LootResolution.cs) · [Scenario overrides](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioCreation.cs)

Recommend two additional Type values, Currency success faces and one fixed amount. Use the existing Type/optional scenario-override precedence. Validate the **resolved** configuration, including Loot plus Currency faces at most six and a positive amount for enabled Currency. With Currency disabled, retain the exact old branch, event payload and random-call schedule. When enabled, roll once. For Loot 1, Currency 2, faces 1 / 2–3 / 4–6 give Loot / Currency / nothing. If every card is reserved or the deck is empty, propose that the Loot face yields nothing and the Currency faces stay fixed. This preserves printed probabilities and permits Currency-only scenarios. Authoring diagnostics should show face ranges, including combinations invalidated by a Loot override. Deck provisioning continues to count only expected Loot and unopened markers. [Drop configuration](ProjectDelve.Engine/Loot.cs) · [Generator finite supply](ProjectDelve.Generation/PopulationLoot.cs)

Emit the configured roll, outcome and former anchor for the new reward path. Emit collection with Cell, collector, Side, amount and resulting balance after both removing the pile and crediting the bank. Render a count on the board and a Side total in the client from the filtered Engine snapshot. Recommend that the observing Side see its own balance, board amounts under the existing explored-Cell policy, and a visible opponent pickup without the opponent's total. Apply the same filtering to pending choices, diagnostics, events, reconnect snapshots and controller observations. The authoritative state and RNG checkpoint already support continuation. Web replay for reconnect is bounded, and the game does not reconstruct state from events. ## Physical levels and effective HP

**Implemented today.** A Unit owns HP, its inventory and native spent-use counts. An `OwnedObject` has a physical ID, typed reference, optional Loot Card provenance and Equipment-local spent-use counts, even on the ground. Abilities bind to a source kind, Type, physical instance and local key. Two Equipment copies already retain independent charges. Effective MOV, RNG, ATK, DEF and sight use multiple Engine paths, including optimized passive lookup for observations. Movement capabilities and some native mechanic queries read Type declarations directly. **Maximum HP currently comes from `UnitType.Hp`.** Healing candidates, healing resolution, scenario setup, restored-state validation, Station normalization and movement presentation all have direct readers. [Physical objects](ProjectDelve.Engine/Loot.cs) · [Ability queries and validation](ProjectDelve.Engine/Abilities.cs) · [Effective stats](ProjectDelve.Engine/Model.cs) · [Observation stats](ProjectDelve.Engine/ObservationStatContext.cs) · [Healing](ProjectDelve.Engine/WorldResolution.cs)

**Proposed authoring.** Make today's declaration Level 1. Give a Type an optional ordered list of higher levels. Each Equipment entry declares its complete effective Ability list. Each Unit entry declares the complete level-dependent base stats and native Ability list. Invariant identity, footprint, uniqueness, controller defaults, drop policy and ownership capabilities remain on the Type. A step specifies its price and any one-time restorations. Resolve one selected entry on demand, then use the ordinary modifier, movement and Ability machinery. Cumulative patches would need ordering, remove/replace semantics and replay of earlier edits. Repeating a short Ability list is cheaper for the first authored families. For example, Lucky Boots could retain the same local key across MOV +1 and MOV +2. The latter means a total bonus of two.

Persist a default-1 level on each Unit and on each Equipment object, including ground objects and carried Loot-derived Equipment. Keep Items single use. Accept an optional initial level on Unit placements and initial objects. Validate level bounds, sequential definitions, costs, mechanics, dependency references and spent counts against the selected level at every entry point. Level changes must leave the shared Type, Activation Token, other copies and scenario-local overrides intact. Route execution, passive fast paths, movement/Phase, AI queries, isolated previews, card inspection and FoW through the same selected configuration. Level-only referenced content must participate in packaging and Expedition refresh. The C# roster and local JSON should author the same types. [Content dependency discovery](ProjectDelve.Engine/ContentDefinitions.cs) · [Movement capabilities](ProjectDelve.Engine/MovementCapabilities.cs) · [Equipment definitions](ProjectDelve.Engine/Equipment.cs) · [Unit precedence](ProjectDelve.Engine/UnitContent.cs)

**Uses and HP require explicit rules.** Recommend preserving spent counts for stable local keys, beginning new keys unspent and clearing only the upgraded source's limited-use counters when the step explicitly restores them. First entries can retain limited keys with nondecreasing maxima, avoiding ambiguous removed or reduced limits. Reject unsupported transitions at authoring. A restoration does not refresh a Bonus Action's once-per-activation budget or replace a consumed Item. Equipment restoration can separately target its final bearer for HP. Name each other restorable resource and its cap before adding that field. Unit restoration affects its native uses. [Source accounting](ProjectDelve.Engine/AbilityAccounting.cs)

Add one authoritative effective maximum HP query usable for both pending placements and live Units. Compute the final level and ownership first, then restore to that **new effective maximum**, including applicable Equipment. For example, an injured Unit going from base 4 to 5 with a final +1 HP Equipment bonus finishes at 6 if the step heals it. Without a heal, recommend preserving current HP up to the new cap. Decide whether a falling cap clamps HP without triggering Damage. The current physical rules permit base HP above one only on Unique Types. Decide how that constraint applies to effective HP from gear on a non-Unique bearer, and whether an HP grant applies while Lying. Prefer an explicit continuous bearer HP bonus with clear Posture behavior before enabling temporary or area-based HP modifiers. Test setup, heal targeting, World healing, preview, observation and restoration with the same query. [Unique rule](CONSTRAINTS.md#unique-unit-types) · [Placement](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioCreation.cs) · [Station normalization](ProjectDelve.Engine/EquipmentStation.cs)

## One Station with Manage, Upgrade and Recruit

**Implemented today.** Selecting an Action-timed DelveMart Operate snapshots the eligible Units and original allocation before spending anything. Participants are living, placed, same-Side inventory-capable Units, including distant, Lying, Dormant and summoned Units. Confirm rebinds the invoking Ability, rechecks Operate, validates the entire allocation and actual terrain occupancy, then applies accounting on a copy. An invoking Item is reserved and consumed on success. Invoking Equipment can move with its newly spent charge. Transaction snapshots show the completed allocation. The browser edits a draft, requests Engine previews and uses session, revision and decision guards. An Expedition checkpoint can carry an invalid intermediate draft. Default automation filters interactive Station choices. [Station lifecycle](ProjectDelve.Engine/EquipmentStation.cs) · [Web preview guards](ProjectDelve.Web/PlaytestGame.Machines.cs) · [Editor](ProjectDelve.Web/wwwroot/equipment-station.js) · [Automation](ProjectDelve.Engine/DefaultAutomatedProvider.cs)

**Proposed transaction.** Put a fixed non-negative base sell value on `ItemDefinition` and `EquipmentDefinition`. Print it on the card and expose it in the Content Viewer/Designer. Give legacy local Types a documented default, for example 1, so they can participate without a catalog rewrite. Manage and Upgrade edit one typed Station draft. Each pooled physical ID gets one final outcome, an eligible owner or a sale. Add next-level steps keyed by Unit ID or Equipment ID. Read prices and opening balance from authoritative state. Opening the Station remains possible with an empty inventory or zero balance, preserving the Expedition arrival operability contract. Recommend that every living placed same-Side Unit with a defined next level can upgrade, including those unable to own objects. Only Party membership determines handoff.

1. Recheck the pending boundary, actor, source and eligible IDs. Reserve the invoking Item outside the pool. Reject duplicate dispositions, unknown objects, sold-and-upgraded Equipment and unavailable levels. Recommend at most one step per individual target per Confirm for the first release.
2. Price sales at opening levels. Compute opening balance + proceeds − all upgrade costs with checked integer arithmetic. A transfer, sale or level change qualifies as a change. Sale-only and upgrade-only are valid. A no-op stays pending. For example, opening 8 plus a sale at 4 pays for a 12 upgrade.
3. On a detached state, account for exactly one Action, the invoking Ability, its costs and any consumed Item. Distribute physical objects with their updated counters, retire sold objects, apply levels, then apply restorations to final owners. For a recharge of the invoking source, propose keeping this Operate use spent against its new allowance. Check final HP, counts, ownership capabilities, one Equipment per Type, balance and all affected footprints. A failed validation discards the whole candidate state. Sold Loot-derived cards never replenish the finite deck.
4. Preview and Confirm use the same calculation. Show sale lines, price per step, starting and resulting balance, source uses, and final owner. Keep existing normalized loadout comparisons, alongside actual projected HP and use changes for restorations. Confirm revalidates the live state and publishes the complete result with stable ordering by physical ID. Cancel only clears the decision. Reset clears every tab's draft. A stale or duplicate submission must never pay or credit twice.

This retains one pending decision and one successful Machine use. The added complexity comes from contested objects and the effect of final ownership on occupancy, HP and uses. Keeping Recruit on its own confirmation bounds that complexity. It can share the Station's function picker. Moving from a dirty Manage/Upgrade draft into Recruit should require committing or explicitly abandoning it. An earlier completed sale may fund recruitment.

## Party, exit and paid recruitment

**Implemented today.** `ExpeditionParty` declares five fixed instance/Type pairs, requires five distinct Types, assigns a reserved Group and human agency, and constructs Reach Area with five specific Unit selectors and Count 2. `ExpeditionWorld.Configure` sets capacity to five. Runner Build, Load and head validation reference the originals. Handoff recreates even defeated originals Upright at full HP, carries only currently owned objects with stable IDs, clears Unit and retained Equipment spent uses, and starts before Round 1. It resolves current whole definitions, falls back to saved definitions when missing and fails on an incompatible refresh. Continue archives the completed checkpoint before publishing the successor and head, with a retry receipt. [Party construction](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionParty.cs) · [World configuration](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionWorld.cs) · [Runner](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionRunner.cs) · [Checkpoint and head](ProjectDelve.Web/ExpeditionStore.cs)

**Proposed model.** Give Expedition game state an ordered Party roster of stable Unit IDs, initialized from the originals and updated atomically with recruitment. An absent Party denotes an ordinary scenario. Store membership once in authoritative live state, from which the checkpoint, objective and handoff read. The initial scenario records its starting roster for deterministic setup. The head can retain founder provenance. At the table, list numbered members on the Expedition sheet and mark levels on each Unit or Equipment card, including Equipment lying on the board. Preserve membership and Unit level after defeat. Use a narrow Party selector on the Expedition Reach Area. Reuse the ordinary living, placed, full-footprint, simultaneous-boundary test with **Count 2**. A recruit qualifies as soon as admitted, at the next ordinary evaluation. Preserve specific Unit, frozen Side At Start and dynamic Group semantics for other scenarios. [Objective evaluation](ProjectDelve.Engine/ScenarioObjectives.cs) · [Selector rules](CONSTRAINTS.md#reach-area-and-defeat-units-objectives)

Build the successor from the committed Party and all Side balances, including a Monster Side with no living Unit or no local deployment in the next region. Keep stable Expedition Side IDs and validate dormant balances against the Expedition's known Sides. Copy member levels and their currently owned physical objects with levels/provenance. Reset uses, HP, posture, activation and exploration on the established arrival boundary, applying the effective maximum after gear and levels are installed. Defeated members return with whatever they still own under ordinary defeat rules. Ground gear, piles, temporary allies, and the historical source balances stay in the source snapshot. Successor balances are opening values, never an additive award on retry. A refreshed definition missing the saved level must fail before head replacement, with a diagnostic naming the member or object.

**Recruitment proposal.** Offer one content-referenced, 1×1, ordinary Land-compatible Type for the sixth slot. The shipped Budget Barbarian is a candidate for testing. Its existing Kit summons a temporary copy, providing a test of membership and Unique conflicts. Fresh recruitment allocates a collision-checked, reproducible Expedition Unit ID, distinct from a summon and stable across successors. Enumerate legal visible placement Cells adjacent to the operator using existing footprint, terrain, Edge/LOS adjacency and occupancy checks. Recheck price, Side, Party size, uniqueness and placement on Confirm. Admit the member, place it and debit the Side atomically, then resolve ordinary arrival effects. A blocked board offers no purchase. Use the existing group/controller assignment rules with an explicit Party agency policy and the ordinary round-bag snapshot. Joining never inserts an extra token mid-round. [Summon placement predicates](ProjectDelve.Engine/SummoningRules.cs) · [Creation path](ProjectDelve.Engine/AbilityExecution.cs) · [Published Unit and Kit](ProjectDelve.Web/Content/Canonical/workspace-publication.bundle)

For an initial ceiling of six, derive successor deployment capacity from the persisted roster before generating geometry. At six, the existing planner needs at least 12 area Cells, a 9-Cell maneuver core, six ordered slots and a departure. Verify the guaranteed arrival Station, first-region exposure protection, unobstructed route and matched seam. Current handoff explicitly supports only 1×1 figures and distinct Type IDs. Keep a roster slot occupied by a defeated Unique member so recruitment cannot create a duplicate that must deploy after restoration. For later non-Unique recruits, deduplicate agency assignments per token rather than enforcing Type uniqueness on the Party model. Size and footprint support are offer/generation limits, independent of theme. ## DelveCare and Meteor-O-Mat

**Implemented today.** Machines are blocking single-Cell objects, public or Side-owned. Operate already checks the Ability source, adjacency through a usable Edge, ownership, per-Machine limits and relevant costs. Existing Resource costs draw from Machine storage. Operation validation, descriptions and `RequiresOutputCell` currently distinguish Resource output, Reveal and Station. A paid service needs an explicit case so a loose Resource on the operator's Cell does not block it. [Machine rules](ProjectDelve.Engine/Machines.cs)

**Recommendation.** Use two concrete typed operation payloads with content-defined offers. Emit an ordinary Operate candidate per legal offer if the recipient is fixed. Include the offer ID in the candidate key and recheck it at resolution. Validate before Action accounting, Side debit, Machine use and effect. Keep previews deterministic. The Machine's owner restricts access, the operator's Side pays, and the recipient follows the offer. Recommend operator-only for both first Machines.

- **DelveCare** can sell a capped 2-HP heal or full heal using the effective maximum HP query. Require a living damaged recipient, preserve Posture and omit no-effect offers. For a recharge, specify whether it restores one spent native use, all native uses, or a named Equipment source. A source-selecting service needs a target choice and physical source ID.
- **Meteor-O-Mat** can dispense the published `last-resort-fireball` Item. It already grants the existing Fireball Action with effective RNG, LOS, area targeting and attack dice. Create one fresh owned Item with a reproducible collision-checked ID and no Loot Card provenance. Its eventual use follows ordinary Item consumption. An Item-capable purchaser may own repeated copies. Existing rules impose no numeric Item capacity. An ineligible recipient or exhausted offer cannot buy, and nothing is charged or dropped on the ground. A stronger fixed-damage meteor would require separately approved Ability text. [Published Item](ProjectDelve.Web/Content/Canonical/workspace-publication.bundle) · [Fireball contract](ProjectDelve.Engine/MechanicCatalog.Abilities.cs)

Start with authored Machine placements. For Expedition world placement, reuse reachable operating-position and route protection checks and a pinned, independent generation policy. Retain the guaranteed arrival Station and its optional public counterpart. Optional service and vending sites can be omitted where placement fails. ## Initial playtest economy

**Evidence.** Across 100 matched first-region seeds the C# calibration found **44.29 hostiles** on average (30–59) and **16.88 initial Loot markers** (3–54). Only 0.68 hostiles on average were within eight Cells of the opening formation in the 25-seed arrival subset. WLD1 uses All/Standard and adds exploration rewards independently of population. The shipped Web additions plus the C# roster give All **35 distinct Loot Cards** before multiplication, of which 30 grant Equipment, three grant Items and two are Immediate. Only owned objects can be sold. These are supply figures. Kills, encounters reached, cards drawn, surplus sold and income actually banked per Side have not been measured here. [Expedition calibration](EXPEDITION_INTRO_RESULTS.md) · [Loot abundance](ProjectDelve.Generation/LOOT_ABUNDANCE_A1.md) · [Base cards](ProjectDelve.Engine/LootRoster.cs) · [Shipped additions](ProjectDelve.Web/Content/Canonical/workspace-publication.bundle)

| Proposed playtest entry | Initial value in Currency |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary enemy, e.g. Grunt | Retain 1 Loot face. Add 2 Currency faces paying 6, expected 2 per defeat before collection. |
| Specialist, e.g. Troll | Retain 2 Loot faces. Add 2 Currency faces paying 9, expected 3. |
| Red Dragon | Retain 3 Loot faces. Add 2 Currency faces paying 15, expected 5. |
| Base sell price per individual Type | Start ordinary Items at 3, Fireball Item at 5, common Equipment at 4, stronger Equipment at 6–8. |
| Equipment and Unit level steps | Pilot Equipment at 12 to Level 2 and 24 to Level 3. Pilot a Unique Unit at 24 and 40. Author only the steps each Type needs. |
| Recruit and vending | Try 75 for the sixth member, 15 for the Fireball Item. If later offering a seventh, try a separate 110 entry. |
| DelveCare | Compare the suggested 10 for 2 HP and 50 for full with 8 and 16. The five starters have 4–5 HP, so full healing at 50 buys mainly Action efficiency. |

**Pacing example.** With zero opening funds, ten ordinary defeats at those odds, 75% of dropped Currency recovered by the Party Side, and two surplus objects sold for 4 each, expected spendable income is **23**. One early Equipment step is affordable, a Unit step needs another coin, and saving 75 takes about 3–4 such regions. These are explicit playtest assumptions. A route that avoids combat, a Monster collecting drops first, or selling a larger share of the Equipment-heavy deck changes the result. Start with base-only resale at every level. If investment recovery feels necessary, try an authored resale increment per level capped at roughly one quarter of its upgrade cost, rounded down. This keeps price visible on a physical card and avoids purchase-history bookkeeping. If actual historical spend must matter across price refreshes, an instance needs a physically recorded basis. Validate purchase price above resale and each resale increment below its step cost.

Finite Loot limits ordinary reward farming today. Currency lacks that brake. Explicitly review repeatable summons on a Type with Currency faces and summoned allies selling transferable gear. First offer Currency only on selected enemy Types and keep free repeatable summon targets at zero where desired through content. Do not infer eligibility from killer, Side or a summon flag. Measure first affordable upgrade, median and low/high income at **exit**, competing Monster pickups, resale share, repeated summon income and regions to recruit, including no-population and Loot-poor routes, before tuning permanent prices.

## Compatibility, implementation order and verification

Current Expedition heads accept versions 1 and 2 and checkpoints require version 1. Checkpoints hold concrete Scenario, Content, live state, RNG position and optional Equipment draft. Introduce versioned readers where semantics change. Verify historical bytes/digest before migrating in memory. Default legacy balances to zero, board amounts to empty, levels to one and Currency faces to zero. Map an old allocation to plain transfers. Derive legacy Party membership from the verified founder IDs and migrate the generated exit selector in the active Expedition's setup and live state. A recruit can then count even when the region began under the old format. Preserve the pending decision, random position, completed result and archived blobs. Newly authored defaults affect new games at their existing catalog resolution boundary. Content publication digests hash serialized definitions, so omitting absent fields or providing a version-aware digest path matters even for seemingly harmless default properties. ## Decisions needed before implementation

1. **Currency timing and visibility.** Approve destination/Stay/setup pickup, hazard-first and same-Cell Currency-before-Loot ordering, physical piles, and who sees each balance.
2. **Defeat dice.** Approve disjoint d6 faces, a fixed amount per Type and an exhausted Loot face yielding nothing. Choose the first eligible Types with repeatable summons in mind.
3. **Level semantics.** Approve complete level configurations, stable Ability keys, spent-use handling, HP-cap behavior for Lying and non-Unique bearers, and the effect of losing maximum HP.
4. **Station settlement.** Approve sale and upgrade as independent qualifying changes, one step per target, restoration on the final bearer, the invoking-source recharge rule, eligible Unit upgrade targets and the resale policy.
5. **Party admission.** Approve the first recruit, six-member initial ceiling, placement and agency, separate Recruit confirmation and Party-scoped exit selector. Confirm the legacy founder migration and next-region capacity contract.
6. **Paid offers and balance.** Select service recipients, recharge scope, vending stock/use limits and initial price sheet. Decide the desired number of regions to the first Unit upgrade and sixth member so playtests can distinguish a pacing problem from ordinary route variation.
